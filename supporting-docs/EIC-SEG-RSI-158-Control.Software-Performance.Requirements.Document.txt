Controls Software Functional and Performance Requirements Document
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This document presents the functional and performance requirements for the Controls Software subsystems of the Electron Ion Collider Project [5.1]. The requirements are managed in accordance with Systems Engineering and Requirement Management Plans [5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.5]. The requirements for all systems in the EIC flow down from the system top level Global Requirements [5.6] to the General and Functional Requirements [5.7, 5.8] and finally to the Performance Requirements Document (PRD). These performance requirements are necessary to integrate these sub-systems as part of the Electron Ion Collider (EIC) Project.

The performance requirements (indicated by SHALL) and guidance (indicated by SHOULD) provided for the design have been defined based upon the reference physics design performed by Brookhaven Science Associates (BSA) and the spatial restrictions of the building which it will reside.

The EIC controls WBS is divided into several subsections, as shown in Figure 1-1. It consists of: Global Design and Development (GDD) for hardware device control, High-Level Applications for control software, Networking, and support for other accelerator subsystems, such as Power Supplies, Instrumentation, RF, vacuum etc. This document focuses on the control software requirements.

2. INTRODUCTION

The EIC (Electron Ion Collider) will be using the yellow ring of RHIC (Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider) as its Hadron Storage Ring (HSR), while building a new Electron Storage Ring (ESR) in the RHIC tunnel. The two rings intersect at several Interactive Regions (IR), where detectors will be installed to record electron ion collisions.  For the injection systems, a new electron injector comprising a Linac and a Rapid Cycling Synchrotron (RCS) will be built outside the RHIC tunnel. The RHIC injection system remains to serve the hadron storage ring. See Figure 2-1 for a schematic.


Figure 2-1 illustrates the overall layout of the EIC. The HSR is shown as the yellow line, the ESR as a wide red line and the RCS as the thin red line.

This brings unique challenges and opportunities for controls.

RHIC was built in the early 90s, the controls mostly made use of the then state-of-the-art technologies. Architecturally, it is a network based distributed accelerator control system with standard two level architecture: The high-level system handles operator interfaces, automatic sequences, alarms, data archiving and electronic logbook etc., while the low-level system directly controls the hardware components like magnets and vacuum systems using mostly VME hardware, with locally-developed ADO (Accelerator Device Objects) applications running on the real-time operating system VxWorks. The system has been successfully running the RHIC accelerator complex for 25 years with careful improvements and upgrades (such as FPGA based LLRF systems) along the way to enhance accelerator performance and support science missions. 
The existing ADO based control system for the hadron injection, which also serves other facilities such as BLIP (Brookhaven Linac Isotope Producer) and still operates during the EIC construction, will remain. 

With about 2 orders of magnitude increase in beam luminosity compared to RHIC, EIC controls need to employ state-of-the-art technologies. On the lower-level device controls, EIC is designing a common hardware platform that includes a modular and scalable FPGA system. The system will be deployed across all EIC machines, providing integrated support for precise timing, LLRF, beam instrumentation, and other subsystems. 

On the software side, the EIC project prompts us to survey the current accelerator control landscape, paying close attention to code maintainability, to satisfy the goal that controls shall support EIC for its expected 30+ years of lifetime. It is realized that a proprietary development such as RHIC controls could present difficulty attracting future engineers with dedicated and specialized skills to maintain and upgrade the control system. The trend of many new facilities across the DOE complex (and around the world) is moving towards open-source collaborations such as EPICS. EPICS has been successfully supporting many accelerator and beamline facilities for over 30 years. It has been continually developed and grown as a world-wide open-source collaboration. Using a client-server and publish-subscribe model, EPICS is a robust, scalable and high-performance distributed control system, with a myriad of device support. EIC made the decision to join the EPICS collaboration and will adopt the EPICS framework for EIC controls.

For the HSR injector controls that have existing interfaces in place, the plan is to develop ADO bridge software to bring the ADO devices to global controls as EPICS PVs enabling the Main Control Room (MCR) to operate these devices in the same manner as existing EPICS-controlled equipment. An ADO bridge prototype has been developed.

3. CONTROLS SOFTWARE DESIGN
3.1 Overview of Design
Figure 3-1 below shows a schematic of the EIC controls software architecture. One of the key components is the Operator Interface subsystem (shown “CS Studio” as example) that serves as the main control console for the MCR and testing workstations. Running in the background are the important control services such as alarm handling, data archiving, save-restore (supports capturing snapshots of control system settings providing capability to restore them on demand), electronic logbook - these services are already built into the existing RHIC controls. EPICS will require a need to adopt or develop the equivalent services. On the client side, support will be provided for high-level physics and modelling applications and bi-directional gateway services to detector system controls.  Communication between client and server  uses EPICS Channel Access (CA) or PV Access (PVA or PVXS) protocols. HTTP protocol will be used extensively to support services. In addition, project support tools such as e-traveler and inventory control (component database) will be supported.  Automatic sequencing control (RHIC TAPE equivalent) and ADO bridge (to serve existing control objects as EPICS PVs) will be developed.

3.2 Design Scope 
The Controls Software design shall meet all performance requirements as given in Section 4, whilst abiding by the constraints below. 

3.2.1 Design Considerations
Constraints:
* The design shall be delivered within the time interval specified in the latest EIC P6 project schedule. 
* The design cost shall meet the proposed EIC cost.
* The design shall be able to meet all relevant regulatory requirements. 
* The design shall comply with all volume and siting constraints within the RHIC tunnel[JJ6].
* The designs shall meet all the interface requirements necessitated by the various systems which interface with the controls system
* All technical specifications which can be used to verify that a primary requirement of the component is met at the design stage shall be identified and called out in the Final Design Review (FDR). 
* All requirements which can be verified during the FDR shall be satisfied or have a plan in place to satisfy the review team so that the requirements can be met prior to final installation.  

3.2.2 Safety and Environmental Considerations
The design of the Controls Software assembly shall demonstrate it will meet all the safety and environmental requirements in line with Brookhaven National Lab (BNL) Standards-Based Management System (SBMS) and the Integrated Safety Management Plan (ISMP) [5.9].

[YZ14R12]EPICS notification of disconnect is immediate, but it takes (default) 30 sec to close the tcp connection, see https://epics.anl.gov/base/R3-14/12-docs/CAref.html

Prior to EPICS R3.14.5 an unresponsive server implied an immediate TCP circuit disconnect, immediate resumption of UDP based search requests, and immediate attempts to reconnect. There was concern about excessive levels of additional activity when servers are operated close to the edge of resource limitations. Therefore with version R3.14.5 and greater the CA client library continues to inform client side applications when channels are unresponsive, but does not immediately disconnect the TCP circuit. Instead the CA client library postpones circuit shutdown until receiving indication of circuit disconnect from the IP kernel. This can occur either because a server is restarted or because the IP kernel's internal TCP circuit inactivity keep alive timer has expired after a typically long duration (as is appropriate for IP based systems that need to avoid thrashing during periods of excessive load). The net result is less search and TCP circuit setup and shutdown activity during periods of excessive load.
